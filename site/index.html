How the ranking works

Each home gets up to three estimates of value, each a join on public records you can check:

  • County, corrected. The county's assessed market value × the median sale-to-assessment ratio of the 30 nearest recorded sales at a similar assessed value. Assessors run low on expensive homes and high on cheap ones, so the raw assessment is never used alone.
  • Comparable sales. Median price per square foot of up to 10 similar homes (same kind, ±25% size, built within 25 years) that closed in the last year within 2 miles, × this home's square feet.
  • Last sale, brought forward. The last recorded deed (if 2+ years ago) × how much that county's prices have moved since, measured against today's assessments.

The score is the average discount across all three (a missing one counts as zero), minus half the disagreement between them. Three estimates that agree beat one big gap.

Not ranked: homes under contract, lots and land, teardowns, new builds the assessment hasn't caught, condo units that couldn't be matched to their own parcel, co-ops, multi-parcel listings, auctions and mobile homes. Listings from Realtor.com; parcels, assessments and deeds from the seven counties via MetroGIS; permits from Minneapolis and Saint Paul.
